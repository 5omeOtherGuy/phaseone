# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We want phaseone to be a project anyone can take part in, whatever their background, experience or identity. People will disagree about technical choices. They should be able to do that without it becoming a personal attack.

## Expected behaviour

- Be respectful, and assume good faith.
- Criticise code and designs, not people.
- Credit the work and ideas of others.
- Keep discussion in the issue, discussion or pull request where it belongs.
- Respect privacy. Do not post other people's private information, and never post credentials, tokens or private prompts.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, insults, personal attacks, or demeaning comments about anyone's identity or characteristics.
- Trolling, or deliberately disruptive behaviour.
- Publishing someone's private information without their consent.
- Sexual content, or unwelcome sexual attention.
- Any other conduct a reasonable person would find inappropriate in a professional setting.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in every project space: the repository, its issues, pull requests, discussions and commit messages. It also applies when a person represents the project in public.

## Reporting

Report a concern privately to the maintainer, [5omeOtherGuy](https://github.com/5omeOtherGuy), through their GitHub profile. Do not report it in a public issue or pull request. Say what happened, where it happened, and link to anything that shows it. Reports are read in confidence. The reporter's name is shared only with their agreement, or where it is needed to act on the report.

## Enforcement

The maintainer reviews each report and decides on a response in proportion to the conduct. Responses range from a private request to change, to a temporary or permanent ban from the project's spaces. Where possible, the maintainer tells the person involved what was decided and why. Anyone may ask the maintainer to review a decision again.

## Changes

This Code of Conduct is written for phaseone and may change as the project does.
